I.

Background
The rapid evolution of globalization has significantly impacted workforce

mobility and industrial development, thereby reshaping the landscape of occupational
health and safety. In recent years, the world has faced increasingly complex global health
challenges, including emergence and re-emergence infectious diseases, the rising burden
of non-communicable diseases, workplace accidents, environmental hazards, and sudden
medical emergencies. These evolving risks necessitate a more adaptive, resilient, and
integrated approach to health systems within workplace settings.
Work environments particularly in sectors such as manufacturing, construction,
energy, transportation, and other labour intensive settings are inherently exposed to a
wide range of potential emergencies and critical conditions. According to the Social
Security Agency for Workers (BPJS Ketenagakerjaan), up to 13,000 workplace accidents
cases were recorded in Batam in 2022, resulted in a significant number of fatalities.
Incidents such as sudden cardiac arrest, traumatic injuries, acute respiratory distress,
chemical exposure, heat-related illnesses, and other life-threatening conditions demand
immediate and effective response. Without adequate preparedness and response systems,
these events can lead to severe consequences, including loss of life, long-term disability,
and decreased productivity.
Importantly, the effects of health emergencies in workplace settings are not
limited to workers alone. Communities living around or interacting with these sectors are
also vulnerable to the repercussions of such incidents. According to the International
Labour Organization (ILO), approximately 2.93 million workers die annually due to
occupational accidents and work-related diseases, while more than 395 million non-fatal
occupational injuries occur worldwide each year, many of which also affect surrounding
communities and public health systems. Recent studies demonstrate that workplacerelated health risks, including infectious disease outbreaks, hazardous material exposure,
and environmental contamination, may extend beyond employees and contribute to wider
community transmission and public health burdens, particularly in densely populated
industrial regions (Overton et al., 2023). Furthermore, occupational safety should be
approached not only as a workplace issue, but also as a broader public health
responsibility due to its impact on surrounding communities and healthcare systems

(Karanikas & Zerguine, 2025). Accidents, hazardous material releases, environmental
contamination, fires, and infectious disease outbreaks can have far-reaching
consequences for nearby populatons. Workers’ families, informal sector participants, and
nearby residents often experience direct or indirect impacts, underscoring the necessity
for a more inclusive and community-oriented approach.
Therefore, to effectively enhance emergency and critical care capacity, efforts
must extend beyond workplace boundaries to encompass surrounding communities. An
integrated system that links occupational health services, community health resources,
emergency responders, and referral networks is vital for ensuring timely and effective
management of health emergencies across various settings.
In light of evolving global health challenges, occupational health should evolve
past a sole focus on preventive and promotive measures. It must also incorporate
preparedness, early detection, rapid response, and comprehensive emergency
management. Recent studies have demonstrated that integrated occupational safety
systems incorporating emergency preparedness and rapid response mechanisms
significantly improve workplace safety outcomes, reduce occupational injuries, and
strengthen workers’ psychological wellbeing and family resilience (Tewari & Paiva,
2022). In addition, risk management frameworks integrated with emergency response
components have been shown to enhance organizational preparedness and long-term
workforce wellbeing (Glevitzky et al., 2025). Enhancing the competencies of
occupational health personnel, workplace first responders, and community stakeholders
is essential in building resilient systems that safeguard both workers and the broader
population.
Given these considerations, there is an urgent need to enhance emergency and
critical care capacity in both workplace and community settings. This initiative seeks to
improve preparedness, foster collaboration among stakeholders, and strengthen integrated
response systems to effectively address current and future health challenges. Ultimately,
these efforts will contribute to safeguarding workers, protecting surrounding
communities, and promoting resilient and sustainable health systems.
As one of the academic pillars of Riau Islands, the Faculty of Medicine at Batam
University envisions excelling in occupational medicine and is committed to advancing

emergency medicine through addressing the aforementioned challenges in an
international conference titled, "Occupational Health Amid Evolving Global Health
Challenges: Strengthening Emergency and Critical Care in Workplace Settings to Protect
Workers and Surrounding Communities". This scientific forum aims to serve as a
platform for sharing the latest evidence-based knowledge, exchanging experiences among
practitioners, and formulating joint strategies to strengthen risk mitigation and emergency
response in industrial environments. This conference will promote cross-professional
understanding on the importance of integrating prevention and emergency response
efforts, that protect both workers and communities amid current and future global health
challenges. We aspire to develop policy recommendations, mitigation strategies, and
working networks that will create safer, healthier, and more resilient work environments
in the face of ongoing crises.
II. Objectives
To strengthen emergency and critical care capacity in workplace and community settings
in order to improve preparedness, response, and resilience against evolving global health
challenges, thereby protecting workers and surrounding populations.
1. To enhance knowledge and awareness of emergency and critical care
principles among workers, occupational health personnel, and
community stakeholders.
2. To improve the capacity of workplace first responders and relevant
personnel in recognizing and managing medical emergencies
effectively and promptly.
3. To strengthen coordination and integration between workplace health
systems, community health services, and emergency referral networks.
4. To promote the development and implementation of standardized
emergency preparedness and response protocols in workplace and
community settings.
5. To encourage multi-sectoral collaboration among stakeholders in
addressing health risks and emergency situations.
6. To support the development of resilient systems that can respond to
both routine emergencies and large-scale public health crises.
7. To foster knowledge exchange, best practices, and innovation in

emergency and critical care within occupational and community
contexts.
